# Passive firing resources

`resources.c` is the bounded C energy and heat kernel for the DOS firing trainer. `resources.h` exposes `ResourceProfile`, `ResourceState`, `resource_load`, `resource_reset`, `resource_tick`, `resource_can_fire`, and `resource_fire`. `assets.py::stage(out)` copies the native getter-derived `RESOURCE.DAT`; call `python3 dos/resources/check.py` to regenerate and qualify it. Docker is used for native linking, sanitizer checks, DOS compilation and DOSBox execution.

The stock profile is a loaded Sparrow with its original outfits and one training Energy Blaster as the firing cost. The stock Sparrow carries Beam Lasers; the Energy Blaster replaces their costs only for this trainer. Native getters yield 4000 battery energy, 1.9 energy per tick, 2.9 passive heat per tick, .00077 heat dissipation, 18600 maximum heat, and per-shot 5.8 energy and 18 heat. The native export retains `ESRESOURCE1` text for inspection; staging converts its seven getter values to an8-byte `ESRES2\0\0` header and seven little-endian binary64 values in the same order. `resource_load` rejects missing, malformed, nonfinite or out-of-range values, including negative capacities and shot costs; signed net generation is allowed; staging checks native source, ship, weapon and outfit data hashes.

A tick clamps energy to capacity *before* generation, dissipates heat, sets overheat only when heat is strictly above maximum, clears it only when heat is strictly below 90% maximum, and generates energy and passive heat only while active. It then clamps both levels nonnegative. This follows `Ship::DoGeneration`. `resource_can_fire` combines the Ship action gate for overheating with `Ship::CanFire`'s resource check; `resource_fire` uses the effect of `Ship::ExpendAmmo` for the Energy Blaster. A shot may push heat above maximum; the overheat flag changes on the following tick. Overheat blocks gun firing in the trainer. The later [propulsion slice](../propulsion/README.md) shares this state with thrust and steering, including overheat drift.

The oracle links unchanged upstream native objects and directly calls `Ship::DoGeneration`, `Ship::CanFire`, and `Ship::ExpendAmmo` on copies of the loaded Sparrow. A test-only header visibility macro exposes the private methods and state to the oracle; no upstream source is changed. Nine scenarios cover sustained stock firing, burst, energy starvation and release, heat overrun and recovery, exact heat thresholds, and energy overflow. Synthetic profiles change only capacity, generation or heat capacity and zero passive heat generation in the heat stress cases. Portable native ASan/UBSan and DOSBox at 16 MiB / fixed 20,000 cycles compare all927 trace rows with exact state and flag matches after binary transfer; the recorded result is in `dos/reports/resource-equivalence.json`.

This slice excludes movement energy/heat, hull and shield repair, status effects, fuel, solar collection, active cooling, ship damage and actual hardpoint cooldown. The oracle uses full-health ships so repair is idle. The passive kernel itself does not spend movement costs; the propulsion caller does so before firing. No claim is made about full-game energy behavior.

The coupled propulsion tests exposed why exact transfer matters: this DJGPP decimal
reader can parse0.5 as0.49999999999999991. A resulting tiny residual energy can
trigger the thrust drag path, causing a visible movement discrepancy despite a
negligible numerical input error. Runtime resource/propulsion profiles and their
comparison fixtures now transfer exact binary64 bits. No epsilon or energy
snapping rule was added to simulation arithmetic.
